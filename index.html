NCERT SECTION 11.5

Photoelectric Effect & Wave Theory of Light

Why the well-established 19th-century electromagnetic wave model failed completely to explain the experimental observations of photoelectric emission.

1. What is the Wave Picture of Light?

  • ▸ 19th Century Success: Wave nature of light was well established because it naturally explained Interference, Diffraction, and Polarisation.
  • ▸ EM Wave Definition: Light is an electromagnetic (EM) wave consisting of oscillating electric (E) and magnetic (B) fields.
  • ▸ Continuous Energy Distribution: Energy is distributed continuously over the entire region of space over which the wavefront extends.
  • ▸ Continuous Absorption Assumption: Free electrons at the metal surface absorb radiant energy continuously as the wave washes over them.

Core Verdict (NCERT)

The classical wave picture is unable to explain even the most basic features of photoelectric emission.

2. Three Fatal Failures of Wave Theory (Point-by-Point for Exams)

High-Weightage 3-Mark Question
Failure #1 Intensity vs Kmax

Kinetic Energy Problem

  • Wave Prediction: Greater intensity (I) → Greater amplitude of E & B fields → More energy absorbed per electron → Kmax should increase with intensity.
  • Experimental Fact: Kmax is completely independent of intensity (depends only on frequency ν).
Contradicts Observation (i)
Failure #2 Threshold (ν0)

Threshold Frequency Problem

  • Wave Prediction: At any frequency, a sufficiently intense beam (over enough time) should give electrons enough energy to exceed work function (φ0). No ν0 should exist!
  • Experimental Fact: A strict threshold frequency (ν0) exists below which NO emission occurs at any intensity.
Contradicts Observations (ii) & (iii)
Failure #3 Time Delay vs Instant

Time-Lag Problem

  • Wave Prediction: Wavefront energy is shared among millions of surface electrons. Energy absorbed per electron/sec is tiny → Takes hours or more to escape.
  • Experimental Fact: Emission is instantaneous (Δt ≈ 10-9 s) even at extremely low intensity.
Contradicts Observation (iv)
Feature / Parameter Classical Wave Theory Prediction Actual Experimental Observation
1. Energy Absorption Continuous over entire wavefront by all surface electrons Discrete 1-to-1 packet absorption by single electron
2. Effect of Intensity on Kmax Kmax increases with increase in Intensity (I) Kmax is independent of Intensity; depends linearly on ν
3. Threshold Frequency (ν0) Should NOT exist (high intensity compensates low ν) Strictly exists (ν ≥ ν0 required for emission)
4. Time Taken for Emission Hours or more (huge time lag) Instantaneous (< 10-9 seconds)

NCERT SECTION 11.6 (PART 1)

Einstein's Photoelectric Equation: Energy Quantum of Radiation

How Albert Einstein (1905) revolutionized physics by proposing that radiation energy is built of discrete packets called quanta of energy (hν).

1. Concept of Energy Quantum of Radiation (1905)

  • ▸ Discrete Energy Packets: Photoelectric emission does not occur by continuous absorption of energy. Radiation energy is built up of discrete units called quanta of energy of radiation.
  • ▸ Energy of Each Quantum: Each quantum of radiant energy carries energy E = hν, where:
    • h = Planck's constant (6.626 × 10-34 J s)  |  • ν = Frequency of incident light
  • ▸ One-to-One Elementary Process: In photoelectric effect, one electron absorbs one single quantum of energy (hν) completely at an instant.
  • ▸ Role of Work Function (φ0): If the absorbed quantum energy (hν) exceeds the minimum escape energy needed (work function φ0), the electron is emitted immediately.
NCERT Equation (11.2) Most Important Formula

Kmax = hν − φ0

Maximum Kinetic Energy = (Energy of Incident Quantum) − (Work Function of Metal)

  • ▸ Why Kmax? Kmax is the kinetic energy of the most loosely bound surface electrons that escape without internal collision losses.
  • ▸ Tightly Bound Electrons: Deeper or more tightly bound electrons lose extra energy before escaping and emerge with kinetic energies less than Kmax (0 ≤ K ≤ Kmax).
  • ▸ Meaning of Intensity in Quantum Picture: Intensity (I) at a given frequency is determined by the number of photons incident per second. Increasing I increases the number of emitted photoelectrons/sec, NOT Kmax!

NCERT SECTION 11.6 (PART 2 — DERIVATIONS & GRAPHS)

Derivations, Stopping Potential (V0) & Millikan's Proof

Complete step-by-step derivations of Equations (11.2), (11.3), and (11.4), how Einstein's equation explains all experimental laws, and Millikan's historic determination of h.

Derivation 1 Eq. (11.2) & (11.3)

Threshold Frequency (ν0) & Alternate Forms

Step 1: By conservation of energy for a surface electron:

Kmax = hν − φ0   --- (11.2)

Step 2: Since kinetic energy Kmax must be non-negative (Kmax ≥ 0), photoelectric emission is possible only if:

hν > φ0  or  ν > ν0

Step 3: At threshold frequency (ν = ν0), Kmax = 0:

ν0 = φ0h ⇒ φ0 = hν0  --- (11.3)

Step 4: Substituting φ0 = hν0 in Eq. (11.2):

Kmax = h(ν − ν0) = hc1λ − hc1λ0
Derivation 2 Eq. (11.4) • Board Favorite

Stopping Potential (V0) & Straight-Line Relation

Step 1: Stopping potential (V0) is related to Kmax by Eq. (11.1):

Kmax = eV0

Step 2: Substitute Kmax = eV0 into Einstein's Eq. (11.2) for ν ≥ ν0:

eV0 = hν − φ0

Step 3: Divide both sides by elementary charge (e):

V0 = (he)ν − φ0e  --- (11.4)

Step 4: Compare with straight line y = mx + c:

  • • Graph: V0 vs ν is a straight line
  • • Slope (m): h / e (Universal — independent of metal!)
  • • Y-intercept (c): −φ0 / e
  • • X-intercept: ν0 = φ0 / h
Experimental Proof Millikan (1906–1916)

How Eq. (11.2) Explains Observations & Millikan's Test

  • 1. Linear Kmax vs ν: Single electron absorbs single quantum (hν). Hence Kmax ∝ ν and is independent of intensity.
  • 2. Threshold Frequency (ν0): Greater φ0 ⇒ higher ν0 (= φ0/h). Below ν0, no emission occurs.
  • 3. Current ∝ Intensity: Intensity ∝ number of energy quanta/area/sec ⇒ more electrons absorb quanta ⇒ higher current (for ν > ν0).
  • 4. Instantaneous Emission: Elementary process is 1-quantum to 1-electron absorption ⇒ instantaneous even at low intensity!

Millikan's Verification (1906–1916):

Aimed at disproving Einstein's equation, R.A. Millikan measured the slope (h/e) of the V0 vs ν line for alkali metals (Sodium, etc.). Using known e, he found h ≈ 6.626 × 10-34 J s, proving Einstein right! (Nobel Prize 1923).

NCERT SECTION 11.7

Particle Nature of Light: The Photon

Complete specifications, Compton scattering confirmation, and the 5-Point NCERT Summary of the Photon Picture of Electromagnetic Radiation.

What is a Photon? (Key Specs)

  • ▸ Evidence from Photoelectric Effect: Light in interaction with matter behaves as if made of quanta or packets of energy (hν).
  • ▸ Energy & Momentum Association: Einstein showed that the light quantum also carries a definite momentum p = hν/c = h/λ.
  • ▸ Why a Particle? Having a definite value of both Energy (E) and Momentum (p) is a strong sign that the light quantum is a particle — later named Photon.
  • ▸ Compton Effect (1924): Particle-like behaviour of light was further confirmed by A.H. Compton (1892–1962) on scattering of X-rays from electrons.
Key Historical Milestones

• 1921: Einstein awarded Nobel Prize in Physics for theoretical physics & photoelectric effect.

• 1923: Millikan awarded Nobel Prize for elementary charge (e) & photoelectric effect.

• 1924: A.H. Compton confirms photon momentum via X-ray scattering.

Summary of the Photon Picture of Electromagnetic Radiation (NCERT 5 Points)

Direct 3/5-Mark Board Question
(i)
Particle Behaviour in Interaction: In interaction of radiation with matter, radiation behaves as if it is made up of discrete particles called photons.
(ii)
Energy, Momentum & Speed: Each photon has:
Energy: E = hν = hc/λ Momentum: p = hν/c = h/λ Speed: c = 3 × 108 m/s
(iii)
Photon Energy is Independent of Intensity: All photons of light of a particular frequency ν (or wavelength λ) have the exact same energy E and momentum p, whatever the intensity may be. Increasing intensity at a given λ only increases the number of photons per second crossing a given area, with each photon having the same energy.
(iv)
Electrically Neutral & Undeflected: Photons are electrically neutral (q = 0) and are NOT deflected by electric (E) or magnetic (B) fields.
(v)
Conservation in Photon-Particle Collisions: In a photon-particle collision (such as photon-electron collision), total energy and total momentum are conserved. However, the number of photons may NOT be conserved — a photon may be absorbed or a new photon may be created.

Quick Formula & Constant Sheet (Ch 11.5–11.7)

Prepared by Hrishikesh M (PGT Physics)

1. Energy of a Photon

E = hν = hc / λ

In eV: E (eV) ≈ 1242 / λ (in nm)

2. Momentum of a Photon

p = E / c = hν / c = h / λ

Speed of photon: c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s

3. Einstein's Photoelectric Eq.

Kmax = hν − φ0 = h(ν − ν0)

Work function: φ0 = hν0 = hc / λ0

4. Stopping Potential (V0)

eV0 = Kmax = hν − φ0

V0 = (h/e)ν − (φ0/e) → Slope = h/e

5. Number of Photons/sec (N)

N = P / E = (P ċ λ) / (hc)

Where P = Power of monochromatic source

6. Fundamental Constants

h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J s

e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C  |  1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
